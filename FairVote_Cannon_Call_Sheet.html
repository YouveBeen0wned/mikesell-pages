Call Prep Brief

Brian Cannon · FairVote Action

Prepared for Mike Sell · FL-CD4 2026 · June 1, 2026

Who you are talking to

Brian Cannon is Director of Advocacy at FairVote. He leads the push for ranked choice voting (RCV) and related structural reforms. He is a double William & Mary graduate (BA 2004, JD 2011) with more than ten years in nonprofit leadership and state policy work.

His signature win came before FairVote. He ran a seven-year campaign in Virginia to curb partisan gerrymandering, which led to a 2020 state constitutional amendment creating an independent redistricting commission. That tells you how he thinks. For him, reform is less about any single election and more about building structures that push elected officials to serve the people who put them there. He is also openly for ending the Electoral College through a constitutional amendment.

The two organizations (and why it matters for your ask)

There are two entities, and the difference shapes what you can ask of each.

  • FairVote is the 501(c)(3). It handles research, education, and limited lobbying.
  • FairVote Action is the 501(c)(4) advocacy arm. It can engage more directly in political advocacy, ballot initiatives, and lobbying.

When the conversation turns to campaign coordination or active support, FairVote Action is the entity built for that work. Keep that distinction in mind when you frame any request.

What FairVote is working on right now (2026)

RCV is in active use and growing. As of early 2026, 49 U.S. jurisdictions use RCV or have passed it for upcoming elections, reaching nearly 14 million voters across 22 states plus Washington, D.C. This year, 17 to 19 cities, counties, and states are using it.

Live 2026 fights worth knowing for the call:

  • Maine used RCV for its June 9 primaries, including an open governor’s race and the Senate primary to challenge Susan Collins.
  • Washington, D.C. used RCV for the first time in its June 16 primary.
  • Michigan has an active campaign (Rank MI Vote) to put RCV on the November 2026 statewide ballot.
  • Berwyn Heights, Maryland delivered the first RCV ballot-measure win of 2026.
  • FairVote Minnesota runs an RCV Democracy Champions program that endorses candidates who back local RCV adoption.

The Florida context (read this before you dial)

Florida banned RCV statewide in 2022. The ban was tucked into a larger elections bill (page 25 of 47) and voided local RCV ordinances, including one Sarasota voters had approved in 2007 with 77 percent support.

Because of the ban, FairVote Action’s near-term work in Florida is about laying groundwork and shifting the conversation, not implementing RCV soon. Go in acknowledging that reality. Cannon himself has said the ban was a setback but that RCV remains “the fastest-growing nonpartisan voting reform across the nation.” That nonpartisan framing fits your country-over-party message well.

Suggested questions

  • Given Florida’s statewide ban, where do you see a realistic path forward for RCV here, and what does the groundwork look like in a ban state?
  • What has the Virginia redistricting fight taught you about moving structural reform in a tough political climate?
  • Which 2026 wins (Maine, D.C., Michigan) carry lessons that travel to Florida?
  • Where does FairVote Action see candidates like me fitting into the longer reform arc, even before the law changes?
  • What would meaningful support look like from your side, and what would you need from a campaign like mine to make that work?

Opening lines (in your voice)

Tie RCV to the 4Ps and a country-over-party frame. Pick what fits the moment; do not read verbatim.

WARM OPEN

Brian, thank you for the time. I have followed the Virginia gerrymandering fight, and what stayed with me was the patience. Seven years to fix a structure most people never see. That is the kind of work I respect.

CONNECT TO THE 4Ps

The way I talk to voters about every problem is the 4Ps. Process is the fix itself. Policy is the law that locks it in. People are the folks closest to the work who should be at the center of it. Purse is where the money comes from and who it reaches. Ranked choice voting lands right in Process. It changes how the choice gets made so the outcome reflects what voters actually want.

COUNTRY OVER PARTY

I am not running to win an argument for one side. I am running to give people a system they can trust. Ranked choice voting is one of the few reforms that asks candidates to earn second and third choices, which means we have to talk to everyone. That is the kind of politics I want to practice.

NAME THE FLORIDA REALITY

I know Florida closed the door on RCV in 2022. I am not going to pretend that is small. So my question is the honest one. In a state that has banned it, what does laying the groundwork look like, and how can a candidate like me help carry that forward?
