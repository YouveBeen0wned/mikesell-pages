Cuts to reverse: what they cost and what they return
Reverse
4 million living Americans were marked deceased. Their Social Security was cut off.

The Social Security Administration (SSA), the agency that sends retirement and disability checks, lost more than 7,000 employees. The backlog for new claims grew 94%. Four million living Americans were incorrectly marked as deceased, with no automatic fix. In FL-CD4, where 18% of residents are over 65, the average wait for a disability decision is now 7+ months. Appeals can take 4 to 5 years. These are people who already paid in. Cost to restore SSA staffing: ~$800M per year.

Reverse
Every $1 spent on IRS enforcement returns $5 to $9. We stopped spending it.

Thousands of IRS agents were cut. The Congressional Budget Office (CBO) shows a $5–$9 return on every $1 invested in tax enforcement. The cuts protect complex, high-income filers most. Florida's share of federal program funding is tied to national revenue. Less collection means less money for Medicaid, schools, and roads in FL-CD4. Restoring enforcement staffing pays for itself within 18 months.

Reverse
The CFPB returned $21 billion to consumers over its lifetime. Now it's shut down.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) was cut from ~1,700 staff to a skeleton crew. Before the cuts, it resolved 20% of consumer complaints filed, including against predatory lenders, debt collectors, and credit bureaus. That rate is now under 1%. In a district where median household income is $62,000, a wrong credit report or an illegal fee can be the difference between qualifying for a mortgage or not. Full restoration costs ~$700M per year. Estimated annual return to consumers: $3–5 billion.

Reverse
USAID is gone. So is the early-warning system for the next pandemic.

Over 90% of U.S. Agency for International Development (USAID) programs were cut, including Centers for Disease Control (CDC) disease surveillance that caught outbreaks before they crossed borders, agricultural programs that built export markets for American farmers, and democracy programs that reduced the conditions that create refugee crises. The annual budget was $7 billion. Less than 0.1% of federal spending. Naval Station Mayport's mission depends on the regional stability that USAID helped maintain.

Reverse
Half of the Department of Education's staff is gone. Duval's schools feel it.

Roughly 50% of Education Department staff were cut. Civil rights enforcement, special education monitoring, and student loan oversight all depend on people who no longer have jobs. In Duval County, where Title I funding supports more than 60% of public schools, reduced federal oversight means reduced accountability for how those dollars are spent. Cost to restore: ~$900M per year.

Reverse
VA benefits staff was cut. The people waiting longest to hear back are veterans.

DOGE cut staff at the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) who handle benefits processing, IT support, and benefits coordination. Northeast Florida has one of the highest concentrations of military families in the country. NAS Jacksonville, Naval Station Mayport, and Blount Island Command are all in or adjacent to FL-CD4. Cutting the staff who process earned veteran benefits is not efficiency. It's a broken promise to the people who served.

Legal guardrails: what needs to be written into law before any future "efficiency" review
Guardrail
Anyone accessing federal databases must have a security clearance and tell Congress first

DOGE operatives accessed Social Security, Internal Revenue Service (IRS), and other federal databases without confirmed security clearances and without the 72-hour congressional notification the law already requires. Whistleblowers at the Social Security Administration (SSA) estimated a 65% chance of a catastrophic data breach. 14 lawsuits were filed across 8 agencies. Fix: security clearance required, agency IG approval required, public annual audit required.

Guardrail
Firing more than 100 federal workers at once requires a 60-day public review

DOGE carried out mass layoffs across dozens of agencies without following existing federal workforce reduction rules. Courts blocked many of them. Fix: any reduction of more than 100 employees in a single agency requires a 60-day review period and a public impact assessment. Workers separated illegally get reinstated with back pay.

Guardrail
Congress voted to spend the money. The president doesn't get to unspend it.

The Impoundment Control Act of 1974 was written specifically to prevent presidents from freezing money Congress already approved. DOGE froze and cancelled congressionally approved funds across education, housing, foreign aid, and research. Fix: Congress should have legal standing to sue when approved funds are frozen. Frozen funds must be released automatically 30 days after a court order with no active appeal.

Guardrail
Government contracts go to the best bid, not to companies run by friends

Multiple contracts were cancelled during DOGE operations and replaced with no-bid awards to companies with financial ties to DOGE-affiliated individuals. Fix: a mandatory audit of every contract cancelled and replaced. Zero tolerance for no-bid awards without independent inspector general certification.

Cuts worth keeping, with conditions
Keep
The federal government runs 600 separate payroll systems. That's fixable.

Decades of agency-by-agency tech buying created real redundancy. Consolidating payroll, HR, and records management saves money without cutting any services. Condition: do it through proper procurement, require competitive bidding, and publish a public dashboard showing actual savings.

Keep
Federal office buildings are sitting empty. Sell them.

The government manages a real estate portfolio where many buildings are 20–30% utilized, especially after remote work became permanent. Selling underused properties is straightforward. Condition: fair-market-value certification on every sale. Proceeds go to deficit reduction, not around the appropriations process.

Keep
Audit overseas programs first. Then cut the ones that don't work.

DOGE was right that some overseas programs hadn't been evaluated in years. The problem was the method: eliminate everything rather than audit first. Condition: do the audit DOGE skipped. Restore programs with clear outcomes and American interest. Permanently cut the ones that can't show either. Publish the results publicly.

What these mean for FL-CD4
$300B/yr
The employer health insurance tax break locks in a system that leaves millions out

The single biggest federal tax break excludes employer-sponsored health insurance from taxable income. This keeps the employer-based model in place and leaves out the self-employed, gig workers, and those between jobs. It costs more per year than the entire Medicaid program. Capping it for high earners is the financing mechanism for a public option that covers everyone.

$180B/yr
Capital gains and carried interest: wealth taxed at half the rate of wages

Long-term investment profits are taxed at roughly half the rate of wages. Hedge fund and private equity fees are compensation for a service. They are taxed as investment gains rather than income. Closing carried interest alone raises $18 billion over 10 years. This is the "make corporations and the wealthy pay their fair share" part of the platform.

$85B/yr
Accelerated depreciation for real estate: the federal code is writing the build-to-rent check

This tax provision lets real estate investors write off properties far faster than they actually lose value. It generates paper losses that offset real income. This is the core financial engine behind institutional landlords scaling rapidly in markets like Duval. Build-to-rent grew 600% in FL-CD4 since 2019. The federal tax code is funding that.

$65B/yr
Like-kind exchange (1031): the flipper's best friend, the first-time buyer's worst enemy

This provision lets real estate investors defer capital gains indefinitely by rolling sale profits into a new property. Properties cycle through institutional hands indefinitely instead of being sold to owner-occupants. Limiting 1031 exchanges to portfolios below a dollar threshold redirects the tax benefit toward smaller investors and first-time buyers.

$40B/yr
Data center tax credits: subsidizing the fewest jobs per dollar spent

Section 48 investment tax credits and bonus depreciation let data centers write off 100% of construction costs immediately. Data centers consume large amounts of land, water, and power. They create very few local jobs relative to that footprint. The platform: stop data center tax breaks.

$30B/yr
Stock buybacks: shareholders first, workers later

Before a 1% excise tax was enacted in 2022, stock buybacks were entirely tax-free. The preference still heavily favors returning capital to shareholders over raising worker pay. The platform: fight for higher wages; make corporations pay their fair share.

$25B/yr
Pharmaceutical tax deductions: why your prescriptions cost more in the U.S. than anywhere else

Tax provisions and offshore accounting let drug companies deduct research costs twice and shift profits to low-tax countries. This is the mechanism behind U.S. drug prices running 2–5 times higher than peer nations. The platform: Medicare drug price negotiation.

Pushback

The serious critique: tax reform only reduces the deficit if Congress doesn't immediately spend the savings. Supply-side economists argue accelerated depreciation and R&D credits drive real investment. The response: the JCT scores reform revenue as real. Critical local gaps
Infrastructure
$2.5 billion project backlog in Duval. The city clears $250M a year.

At the current pace, the backlog never gets fixed. $800 million is already authorized but not applied for. Nassau and St. Johns have their own road, water, and stormwater backlogs. Federal RAISE grants, infrastructure law carryover, and EPA septic-to-sewer funds are available. The gap is in applying for them.

Housing
$12M for affordable housing in a $2B budget. One in 10 new homes is built for corporate rental.

Build-to-rent grew 600% in Duval since 2019. The trend is spreading into Nassau and St. Johns. HUD's HOME and CDBG programs are the only federal counter-pressure. The district consistently under-applies for both.

Public Safety
Violent crime rate: 12.43 per 1,000 in Duval, about 1 in 80 residents

JSO and JFR budgets together exceed $1 billion. Federal community violence intervention grants and mental health diversion grants are available but underutilized across the district's federal grant portfolio.

Education
COVID school relief is gone. Nothing is replacing it.

Florida used 90% of its $14.6 billion in pandemic education emergency funds. That cushion no longer exists. Title I and CTE workforce funding across Duval, Nassau, and St. Johns now face flat or declining appropriations with no state backstop in place.

These are first-term jobs. Write the grant applications. Change the authorization language. Require the small business set-asides. The $800 million sitting unspent in Duval's infrastructure pipeline doesn't need a new law. It needs someone in Washington who shows up and asks for it. That is what a Purse-focused representative does on day one.
