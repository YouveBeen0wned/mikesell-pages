OBBBA cuts & risk — Florida's exposure
Florida is the most exposed state in the nation — 4.7M ACA enrollees, 1.4M at risk of losing coverage, and a first-ever SNAP cost shift to states

What the audit found: Florida's OBBBA healthcare risk is not primarily Medicaid cuts — it is the expiration of ACA enhanced subsidies. Florida has 4.7M ACA enrollees, the highest in the nation. Of the ~1.5M Floridians projected to lose coverage, approximately 1.4M (93%) lose it through the ACA Marketplace, not Medicaid. Florida did not expand Medicaid, so OBBBA's Medicaid work requirements — which hit expansion states hardest — have limited direct budget impact here. The real story is premium shock hitting working families. Source: KFF / Florida Policy Institute (Aug 2025).

Tax expenditures — the invisible $1.9T budget
More than all discretionary spending combined — and it mostly flows to the top 20%. Reforming it is not new spending; it is redirecting existing public dollars toward people who need them.

The hidden budget: The federal government spends roughly $1.8–2.0 trillion annually through the tax code — deductions, credits, exclusions, and preferential rates that never appear in the appropriations process. This "invisible spending" is larger than all discretionary spending combined. Unlike appropriations, tax expenditures are largely permanent and uncapped, and they disproportionately benefit corporations and high-income households. Reforming them is not new spending — it is redirecting existing public dollars toward people who actually need them. Source: JCT / OMB Analytical Perspectives.

What these mean for FL-CD4
$300B/yr
Employer health insurance exclusion — locks in the broken system

The single largest tax expenditure subsidizes employer-sponsored health insurance by excluding it from taxable income. This entrenches the employer-based model, leaves the self-employed and gig workers uncovered, and costs more annually than the entire Medicaid program. Capping or reforming this is the funding mechanism for a public option. Platform alignment: federal guarantee of health insurance for all Americans.

$180B/yr
Capital gains preference + carried interest — the wealth accumulation engine

Long-term capital gains and qualified dividends taxed at half the rate of wages. Carried interest — hedge fund and private equity profits — taxed as capital gains, not income. Closing carried interest alone scores $18B over 10 years. Taxing capital gains as ordinary income for incomes above $1M scores far more. Platform: corporations and the wealthy paying their fair share.

$85B/yr
Accelerated depreciation for real estate — subsidizing the landlord class

Cost Recovery / ACRS provisions let real estate investors write off property far faster than it actually depreciates, generating paper losses that offset real income. This is the core mechanism behind institutional landlords scaling rapidly in markets like Duval. Build-to-rent grew 600% since 2019 in FL-CD4 — the federal tax code is writing that check. Platform: end price gouging in rent; end corporate welfare.

$65B/yr
Like-kind exchange (1031) — the flipper's best friend, the renter's worst enemy

Allows real estate investors to defer capital gains indefinitely by rolling profits into new properties. The result: properties are never sold to owner-occupants; they cycle through institutional hands. Limiting 1031 to like-kind property below a dollar threshold redirects the tax benefit toward smaller investors and first-time buyers.

$40B/yr
Data center + energy tax credits — subsidizing the fewest jobs per dollar

Section 48 investment tax credits and bonus depreciation allow data centers to write off 100% of construction costs immediately. Data centers consume enormous land, water, and power — and create very few local jobs relative to their footprint. This is public subsidy for private infrastructure. Platform: stop data center tax breaks; regulate exploitative technology.

$30B/yr
Stock buyback preference — wages last, shareholders first

Before the 1% excise tax enacted in 2022, stock buybacks were entirely tax-free. The preference still heavily favors returning capital to shareholders over wage increases — because wages are taxed immediately while buyback gains are deferred or taxed at preferential capital gains rates. Platform: fight for higher wages; make corporations pay their fair share.

$25B/yr
Pharmaceutical IP deductions — why drug prices stay high

Section 199A and offshore IP structuring allow drug companies to shift profits to low-tax jurisdictions, then deduct R&D twice — once for the expense, once for the IP transfer. This is why drug prices in the U.S. are 2–5x higher than peer countries while companies report offshore profits. Platform: end price gouging in prescriptions; Medicare drug price negotiation.

Pushback

The most serious critique: closing tax expenditures reduces the deficit only if Congress doesn't simultaneously appropriate the savings — "redirecting" requires two bills, not one. Supply-siders argue accelerated depreciation and R&D credits drive investment and growth, and that taxing capital gains as ordinary income would reduce investment and ultimately hurt workers. The response: the JCT scores reform revenue as real; the investment argument applies to productive capital, not the financial engineering (carried interest, 1031 rollovers, buybacks) targeted here.

Healthcare — Invest

National paid leave — federal framework

Economic cost: $500B/yr in lost productivity

Pushback

Food security — Defund / Redirect

Eliminate SNAP work requirement age expansion

800K seniors cut nationally

OBBBA raised the work requirement age from 54 to 64. This cuts food assistance from hundreds of thousands of older adults who cannot realistically meet employment thresholds. Rolling back this provision redirects those benefit dollars back to the people who earned them.

Pushback

Conservative critics argue SNAP work requirements reduce dependency and save taxpayer dollars; the CBO scores savings, not just cuts. The response: the work requirement age expansion specifically targets adults 55–64 who face structural employment barriers — this is not work-incentive policy, it is benefit denial by actuarial targeting.

Economic security — Tax Reform

Tax stock buybacks, redirect to wage tax credits

$30B/yr preference eliminated

The current 1% buyback excise tax is insufficient. A meaningful buyback tax — paired with a wage investment credit for companies that raise worker pay — redirects capital from shareholder enrichment to the workforce that earns it. Platform: fight for higher wages for working families.

Economic security — Tax Reform

Close carried interest — tax wealth like work

$18B over 10 years (CBO)

Hedge fund and private equity managers pay capital gains rates on compensation that is functionally a fee for services. Closing carried interest is a surgical reform: it affects fewer than 10,000 taxpayers and raises $18B over a decade. Platform: make corporations and the wealthy pay their fair share.

Pushback

Critics will argue that federal contractor wage floors raise contract costs, ultimately paid by taxpayers, and that "ending corporate welfare" is a slogan without a specific bill. The response: DoD wage requirements have been implemented multiple times by executive order without measurable contract cost inflation; specificity is in the platform — universal background checks, carried interest closure, data center credit elimination are all named provisions.

Restore CDC gun violence research funding

~$15M/yr — public health Purse

The Dickey Amendment effectively banned CDC gun violence research for 20 years. The Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2020 partially restored it. Fully funding CDC and NIH gun violence research treats gun violence as the public health crisis it is — and generates the evidence base for every downstream intervention. Platform: commonsense gun legislation.

Pushback

Second Amendment advocates will say NICS funding is a backdoor to a federal gun registry, and that CDC research is inherently biased toward gun control conclusions. The response: NICS is a point-of-sale check system with no registry function by statute; CDC research on gun violence produces the same public health evidence base applied to car crashes and opioids — the outcome should follow the data.

Cannabis & justice
Cannabis — Tax Reform / Redirect

Federal legalization = tax revenue + banking access

$8–10B annual federal tax revenue (RAND est.)

Federal prohibition forces legal state-licensed cannabis businesses to operate in cash, without banking, paying an effective tax rate of 70%+ under Section 280E. Federal legalization unlocks banking access, creates a taxable revenue stream estimated at $8–10B annually, and redirects that revenue from black markets. Platform: federal legalization of recreational cannabis; home growing rights for registered medical patients.

Veterans — Reclaim

Restore VA benefits for medical cannabis patients

Affects 40K+ FL veterans with state MMJ cards

Veterans who use state-legal medical cannabis risk losing VA benefits under federal prohibition. Florida has over 40,000 registered medical marijuana patients who are also veterans. Federal rescheduling or descheduling resolves the conflict. Platform: veteran health access; federal cannabis legalization.

Pushback

Opponents will argue cannabis legalization increases youth access and public health costs, and that federal revenue projections are speculative. The response: youth cannabis use has not increased in states that legalized; the public health cost argument applies more forcefully to alcohol and tobacco, both federally legal and taxed. Revenue projections are from RAND, not advocacy groups.

Technology & digital rights
Technology — Defund / Redirect

Stop data center tax breaks

$40B/yr federal + state tax expenditures

Data centers receive substantial federal and state tax incentives — accelerated depreciation, energy credits — despite creating few local jobs relative to their footprint and resource consumption. Eliminating these redirects public dollars toward higher-employment, higher-wage investments. Platform: stop data center tax breaks.

Technology — Defund / Redirect

Regulate exploitative technology — reclaim public cost

FTC enforcement investment

Unregulated data harvesting, algorithmic pricing, and predatory digital platforms impose real costs on consumers and communities. Federal investment in FTC enforcement, digital rights protections, and algorithmic accountability shifts the public cost of exploitative technology back to those who profit from it. Platform: reign in exploitive technologies and protect digital rights.

Technology — Invest

Regulate 5G tower siting — restore local authority

FCC spectrum + local cost-sharing

Federal preemption of local 5G siting authority has removed community input on tower placement near homes and schools. Restoring local regulatory authority costs nothing federally — and gives the community a say in how public spectrum is used and who bears the cost of deployment. Platform: regulate 5G tower placement.

Pushback

Industry will argue that data center tax credits support AI infrastructure and domestic competitiveness against China, and that 5G regulation slows broadband deployment. The response: competitiveness arguments don't justify unlimited public subsidy for private infrastructure; 5G siting regulation is about placement decisions near homes and schools, not deployment speed.

Fix the system — accountability & representation
Accountability — Defund / Redirect

End dark money — public campaign finance

$15–20B in dark money since Citizens United

The federal budget writers are chosen by the same donors who benefit from the tax code they write. Getting money out of politics is the prerequisite for every other Purse reform on this list. Federal small-dollar matching programs and disclosure requirements don't cost the public — they cost the donors who currently own the process. Platform: get money out of politics completely.

Accountability — Reclaim

Independent redistricting — unlock competitive representation

Federal earmark and grant competition

Gerrymandered safe districts concentrate federal appropriations in politically safe hands and reduce competition for earmarks and grants. Federal legislation requiring independent redistricting commissions has a direct Purse effect: competitive districts produce members who fight harder for their constituents' share of federal dollars. Platform: create fair district maps through independent redistricting.

Pushback

First Amendment advocates (from both left and right) argue campaign finance limits restrict political speech under Citizens United, and that independent redistricting commissions introduce their own forms of bias. The response: disclosure requirements and small-dollar matching don't restrict speech — they amplify voices that can't write seven-figure checks; and independent commissions, while imperfect, have produced measurably more competitive maps than legislative self-drawing in every state that has adopted them.
