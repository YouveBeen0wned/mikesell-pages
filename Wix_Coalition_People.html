The other half of the job

The Revolving Door: Who Regulates the Industry They Came From

Backing the groups above is one half of the work. The other half is replacing officials who walked from an industry into the government office with authority over that same industry.

Every entry below records four checkable facts: the office, what it controls, where the person worked before, and where those two overlap. Read them and draw your own conclusion.

What this is not. Nothing here says anyone took a job in order to help a former employer, or that anyone is corrupt, or that anyone intends anything. That is not a softening. It is what makes the file usable. A claim about someone's motive is an opinion that invites a denial. A documented career path is a fact that invites an answer.

Two questions, kept separate. A conflict asks whether a person has authority over a company or sector they recently worked for. A qualification gap asks what the role normally requires and what this person brings to it. They are different questions, and someone can score high on one and low on the other. Of the 39 people here, 24 have no qualification gap at all. Many are genuinely credentialed and also carry a real conflict. Both facts are recorded.

The standard we would apply

A five year cooling off period for all senior federal officials. Real time public disclosure of executive branch conflicts of interest. An independent ethics office with real subpoena power. Ethics leadership chosen for enforcement records, not connections. Current federal cooling off periods run one to two years for most officials.

How this was checked. A published advocacy tracker supplied the starting names. Each was then checked one at a time against Senate confirmation records, federal ethics filings, agency biographies and reporting. Roughly a third of the tracker entries had the wrong prior employer, one person had no connection to the industry claimed, and one had left the role. Sixteen claims failed the check or needed correction and are not published here. Where an ethics agreement, recusal or waiver could not be located, the entry says so rather than implying none exists. Every entry was current on September 13, 2026. Appointments move, so recheck before quoting one.
